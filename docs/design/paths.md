# The silhouette of a path (P51)

Built 2026-10-17. `rz_paths_build`, `rz_paths_recompose` in `src/lego.c`;
`razar paths [path]`.

> In the concept map "el" is joined to "perro", to "es", to "un", to "animal",
> and the silhouette of what it forms gives weight to a construction like it;
> it is taken apart and put together again in other examples where there is
> no "animal" but there is "mamífero", and so it understands — because it is a
> connected path, with pieces more important than others: some are used as
> glue in many forms, the others belong to the concept map. — the author

## What it is

- **A silhouette** is a run of 3 to 5 pieces with two of them left as holes:
  *el _ es un _*. Heard with two or more different pairs in its holes, it is a
  construction. The pairs it was put together with are its **weight**.
- **Glue and content** are not named. Every piece falls in many holes by the
  geometry of the runs alone, so it is the **share** that tells: glue stands
  fixed more often than content. In the little world *el* 0.53 and *un* 0.97
  against *perro* 0.39 and *animal* 0.38.
- **Mates**: two pieces that filled the same hole beside the same piece in the
  other hole, in two or more silhouettes. *animal* and *mamífero*, each with
  *perro* in *el _ es un _*.
- **Put together again**: a mate stands where its mate stood, so a path never
  said is built from one that was. Heaviest silhouettes first.

## In the little world

The text says the dog, the cat, the mouse and the fish are animals and
mammals, and that the horse is a mammal. Nothing says the horse is an animal.

*caballo* and *gato* are mates (they share *el _ es un mamífero* and
*el _ come _*). *gato es un animal* was said, so **caballo es un animal** is
put together, in the place of *gato* — never read, built from the shape.

On the way: with only a few words, the last letters always close a word, and
the marks of P33 took them for closing marks (*gato* read as *g + ato*). The
little world got more words, as any real text has; the weakness stays open
(D117).

## On the New Testament — not yet

Learning on the first half, judged on the second:

- **The heaviest silhouettes are glue**: *_ , _*, *_ . _*, *_ de _*,
  *_ y _*. A run of three with one fixed piece is put together with thousands
  of pairs; *el _ es un _* can never weigh like that.
- **Glue and content by share** pick out rare words on the NT (*rechinar*,
  *hipocresía*): the share is noise where a piece is seen in few silhouettes.
- **Putting together again** first measured 1.18 % against 2.09 % blind — but
  **the bar lied**: the paths it built had to be new to the first half, and
  the blind ones did not, so the blind were mostly common paths the second
  half says again. Corrected below.

## Collect different forms (D115, answered 2026-10-27)

> It must not capture the same form because it hears it more times; it must
> collect different forms. — the author

What a silhouette weighs was the riddle: counted by the pairs that filled it,
*_ , _* weighed thousands and *el _ es un _* could never weigh like that.
The author's answer moves the weight off the silhouette entirely:

- **A form counts once.** The strength of two mates is the number of
  **different silhouettes** that make them mates — not how many partners or
  times in one of them. Counted per partner, *animal*/*mamífero* had 947 in
  the little world; as different forms, 524.
- **The association that collected the most forms puts paths together
  first**, wherever it stands.

On the New Testament, with the bar made honest (the blind paths must be new
to the first half too):

| | Said in the second half |
| --- | --- |
| put together by mates | 2,790 of 143,001 — **1.95 %** |
| blind, the brought piece swapped | 1,478 of 100,688 — **1.47 %** |

1.33× over blind — the first reading of P51 on real text that beats chance.
What it gets right is still glue: the strongest mates are *y* and *,*,
because glue also collects many different forms (each fixed piece beside it
makes a new one).

## Stickers: one of each, and its variants (2026-10-27)

> A collector with a repeated sticker has no use for it; he keeps one, and
> goes for the new forms, unexplored, for the sticker he cannot get. And
> inside each sticker its variants -- as modulations of the same song, not as
> another song. — the author

Counted as silhouettes, *_ , que*, *_ , por*, *_ , no*… gave *y* and *,* 3,519
forms: one sticker sung in 3,519 keys.

- **A variant is discovered, not declared.** Two silhouettes of the same shape
  that differ in one fixed piece are one sticker when those pieces are mates.
  Mates are counted by stickers, so it turns until a round joins nothing;
  joins are never undone, so it ends. The only number is the anecdote rule.
- **Little world**: 2,648 silhouettes become 231 stickers; *animal*/*mamífero*
  share 43 stickers, not 524 forms.
- **New Testament**: 137,322 silhouettes become 10,441 stickers. The glue
  shrinks — *,*/*y* from 3,519 forms to 419 stickers — and is still the
  strongest pair (then *,*/*.* 293, *,*/*;* 202). The bar does not move
  (1.95 % against 1.47 %): the 200,000 paths it builds still all come from the
  strongest glue pairs.
- **Chained variants melted the stickers into blobs**: the two largest held
  10,236 and 10,004 silhouettes — *_ , que _* in the same sticker as
  *_ de aceite _*. Each swap was a fine modulation; their sum was another song.

### The structure is a meaning of its own (the author's answer)

> We must tell connectors from meanings, structure from meanings. The
> structure is a meaning in itself: it says something about what it carries
> inside; the meanings outside it give sense to the meaning of the structure.
> First recognise the meaning of the structure, then the meaning of what it
> carries. It is the song played by different instruments: we cannot confuse
> a drum with a piano. — the author

Telling connectors by their share of fixed places fails on the NT: a frequent
piece stands fixed in many silhouettes just for being frequent — *es* 0.44 and
*hombre* 0.39 nearly tie, and the cut the text makes calls *Dios*, *casa* and
*pan* connectors. So no piece is classified beforehand. **The structure of a
sticker is what every one of its variants keeps in the same place**, and two
stickers join only if, joined, they still keep a piece in common. A chain can
no longer drift: to reach *_ de aceite _* from *_ , que _* nothing would be
kept.

- **New Testament**: 31,600 stickers; the largest holds 701 silhouettes, not
  10,236. *_ , que _* now sits in a sticker of 113 variants, all *_ X que _*:
  *necesario que*, *sabían que*, *impedir que*. A sticker kept by *eres*:
  *eres _ Hijo _*, *eres _ Mesías _*, *eres _ de _* — "you are the Son, the
  Messiah".
- **The bar does not move** (1.95 % against 1.47 %): the strongest mates are
  still glue — *,*/*y* now share 2,333 stickers, because glue really does stand
  in many different structures.
- **Not built yet: the sticker he cannot get.** Glue is everywhere, so it
  really is in many different stickers; what the collector wants is the one
  missing from his album. That is the next riddle.
