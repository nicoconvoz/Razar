# The instruments of a word (P43)

Built 2026-10-11. `rz_word_instruments` in `src/lego.c`, `razar instruments <word> [path]`.

> There can be diverse structures — "es" is not used for one single thing, there
> are variants in its use. It has to break down the musical track and know when
> a bass plays, or a guitar, a piano, drums or the voice. … The music becomes
> complex in many pieces, but it is one song that defines it. We can use the
> mixed tracks to see which instrument resonates with the note, and then
> separate the track to study well what it is and what it does. — the author

## Why

"es" paid −0.028 as a way of asking on the NT (P42): what stands before it is
"que", "lo", "Este", ", " — because "es" defines, points, restates, compares,
asks, all at once, and Razar heard one instrument. The word is one type; each
time it sounds it may be a different token — the type-versus-token tension of
the original AMMC, at the level of use.

## How

1. **The mix.** Every time the word sounds, its timbre is the company of the
   word just before it and of the word just after it (P33's company vectors),
   side by side.
2. **Separating.** Two-means on the half of the text it splits on (unit
   centres), then the same again inside each side, only while it pays.
3. **The judge**, on the other half, two questions:
   - *Another instrument, or another player?* On each side of the word, the
     commonest neighbour of one group either is, or is in the silhouette of, the
     commonest neighbour of the other — "rex" and "tom", "a" and "the" — or it is
     not ("what" beside "rex"). Only a change of kind on some side is a new
     instrument.
   - *Does it explain the timbre?* The two centres must explain the held timbres
     better than the single centre, by more than each of eight random splits of
     the same half judged the same way.
4. **Resonance.** An occurrence goes to the centre it resonates with most.
5. **Studying each track:** what sounds before and after it more than around the
   word as a whole.

## In the little world

"rex **is** a dog" says what rex is; "what **is** rex" asks it. "is" plays
**two** instruments — before: *what* | after: *rex tom lou kit*; and before:
*names* | after: *a one the*. Every "what is" resonates with the one that asks.
"ran" plays **one**.

## On the New Testament

"es", 1,032 times, 14 instruments. The ones that read plainly:

| Instrument | Before | After | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| describes a quality (201) | Dios, Ley, hombre, fe | fiel, bueno, verdadero, veraz | "Dios **es** veraz" |
| presents (49) | «Este, «Esto | mi, el, tu | "«Este **es** mi Hijo muy amado»" |
| asks who (20) | «¿Quién, «¿No | este, uno | "«¿Quién **es** este?»" |
| says whose (41) | que, no | de, del, por, para | "lo que **es** del César" |
| what for (27) | ojo, saludo, Señor | para, de, como | "tu ojo derecho **es** para ti" |
| rhetorical (32) | ¿No, ¿Cómo | posible, acaso, necesario | "¿No **es** posible…?" |
| defines (91 + 31 + 62) | Cristo, Jerusalén, Jesús | la / el | "porque **es** el trono de Dios" |

"Defines" came out in three pieces, split by what follows — "**es la**…" and
"**es el**…": gender, not a different instrument. "el" and "la" are not in each
other's silhouette because their company differs by gender (the same reason
*madre* did not sit with *padre* in P37).

## P44 — The same song in another key (2026-10-11)

> Notice they share many letters in common, and the structure is very similar.
> What it must detect to fix this: know when it is masculine, when it is
> feminine, and when the rule is for both. — the author, answering riddle 11

`rz_lexicon_keys`, `rz_keys_same`; used by the instrument judge (`alike`).

1. **Twins:** words of the same length that differ in one letter at one place,
   seen twice.
2. **The property:** the difference that repeats across the most twins. On the
   NT, without being told: the final **a/o** — 256 twins (una/uno, toda/todo,
   hija/hijo, esta/esto).
3. **Markers:** the words that stand before twins, split in three by the exact
   optimum — before one side only, before both, before the other only.
4. **One note in two keys:** a marker of one side and one of the other that
   stand **after** the same words, each the other's best.

On the NT:

| | Marks |
| --- | --- |
| el, un, este, las | one side |
| la, una | the other |
| **su, mi, de, los** | **both** — the rule for both |
| el ↔ la, un ↔ una | one note in two keys (51 such pairs) |

### Two lessons from getting it wrong

- **Twins judged by their company lost the real twins.** The first version kept
  only twins of similar company, and dropped gato/gata while keeping toma/tomo:
  the key is exactly what changes a twin's company ("el gato", "la gata").
  Twins are read by their letters; the structure is read in the markers.
- **Partners matched by what follows never met.** Most nouns have no twin ("el
  trono", "la Ciudad"), so "el" and "la" are followed by different words even
  once twins are merged. The key changes what follows a marker, not what comes
  before it — matched by what stands before, el and la paired at once.

### What did not change: "es" still splits by gender

The instruments of "es" on the NT are still 14, with "es la…" apart from "es
el…". The split that separates them is made higher up, where what stands before
is "**esta** es la…" against "**este** es el…" — and este/esta differ by **e/a**,
not by a/o. Gender in Spanish is sung in more than one key (este/esta, ese/esa,
el/la), and Razar found one. Also only the commonest neighbour of each side is
compared (D98).

## Lessons that came from getting it wrong

1. **The two-means collapsed.** Centres kept as raw sums: the larger group's sum
   is longer, and every occurrence fell to it. The first run split nothing. Unit
   centres fixed it.
2. **Judging by exact neighbours splits by player.** The first judge asked
   whether knowing the group told which exact word stood alongside — and split
   "rex is" from "tom is" until the cap of 16. Two players, one instrument.
3. **Judging by explained timbre still splits by player.** Every "rex" keeps
   exactly the same company, so a group of rexes is perfectly tight and always
   beats chance. What separates instruments is a change of **kind**, and kind is
   what the silhouette already measures.

## Open

- **D96** — *partly answered by P44:* el/la and un/una are one note in two keys,
  and masculine, feminine and both are told apart. Still open as **D99**: more
  than one key — the twins of other differences (e/a: este/esta, ese/esa) whose
  sides fall in line with the sides el and la already mark are the same property
  sung another way.
- **D97** — each instrument its own plan (P42) and its own melody (P39): ask
  "Dios es ?" with the instrument that describes, not with all of "es".
- **D98** — only the commonest neighbour of each side is compared; an instrument
  whose two groups share their commonest neighbour but differ in the rest is
  missed.
