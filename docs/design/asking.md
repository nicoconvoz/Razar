# Asking every word (P50)

Built 2026-10-17. `rz_ask_every_word`, `rz_used_together`, `rz_one_piece` in
`src/lego.c`; `razar asks [path]`.

> It's easy: let it ask about every word. The child asks about "el" as much as
> about "perro", and learns the differences, how they join, and how they are
> used together. — the author, answering riddle 1 (D76)

Riddle 1 searched for the word worth asking about. There is none to choose:
every word gets the same questions, and **the shape of its answers is its
kind**.

## The questions

Answered by the map of associations (P47) and by the company a word keeps.
Each answer must be said twice; once is an anecdote. Fallen links (P48) are
thoughts, not answers.

| Question | It answers when the word… |
| --- | --- |
| of | is the thing spoken of (perro —es→ animal) |
| about | is what is said of another (rex —es→ **perro**) |
| relates | is the relation (perro **—es→** animal) |
| before / after | stands before or after other words |

- **Used together**: a pair of shapes side by side, heard across two or more
  different pairs of words, so it is the shapes that are heard and not one
  pair.
- **One piece**: two words where one brings nothing to the map that the other
  lacks. "el perro" says what "perro" says. Two words with the same shape are
  twins, not one piece.

## In the little world

| Shape of the answers | Words |
| --- | --- |
| company only | el, la, un |
| spoken of, said of others | perro, gato, perra, animal, madera |
| relates | es, de, vive |
| spoken of only | mesa, rex, olor |

Nothing named an article or a thing. "el perro" is one piece; "perro es" is
two.

## On the New Testament

Learning on the first half, judged on the second:

- **The articles came out by themselves**: la, el, del, al, un, una get only
  company, with había and ha.
- **The glue answers everything**: ".", "de", "y", "que" are spoken of, said of
  others, and relate — the glue of the map (D106, D110) comes straight through
  into the kinds.
- **The bar says nothing yet.** Of 11,338 pairs the second half says and the
  first never did, 75.8 % fall in a pair of shapes it learned to use together;
  blind (the same words, the second word shuffled) 75.7 %. With five yes/no
  questions there are few shapes, the pairs of shapes cover almost everything,
  and coverage tells nothing.

## Open

- **D113** — a shape is which questions got an answer, yes or no. The answers
  themselves (*what* is said of a word, *what* it stands before) are the
  differences the author asked for, and they are not used yet.
- **D114** — one piece is judged on the map's yes/no as well; on the NT it
  shows pairs like "resucitó no". It should come from the answers too.
