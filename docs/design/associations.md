# The map of associations (P47)

Built 2026-10-12. `rz_assoc_build`, `rz_assoc_reach`, `rz_assoc_traits` in
`src/lego.c`; `razar map <word> [other] [relation] [path]`.

> You are doing it statistically. I don't want it statistical, I want it
> associative. — the author

## The turn

Everything from P33 to P46 grouped words that look alike on average: cosines,
k-means, optimal splits of distributions. The author's correction: understanding
is **chaining what was said**. "The dog is an animal" and "the animal lives":
walking dog → animal, the dog lives. The deep property is not computed, it is
**reached**. This is the AMMC's own idea — a concept map — taken literally.

Statistics stays only as the bar that grades, never as the way of understanding.

## Links

Every statement becomes a named link, told by the markers Razar found itself
(P44):

| The text says | The link |
| --- | --- |
| "el perro **es** un animal" | perro —es→ animal |
| "la mesa **es** de madera" | mesa —es→ madera (de is a marker too) |
| "la madera **es** dura", "Dios **es** amor" | madera —es→ dura (said bare) |
| "el animal **vive**" | animal —vive (an act) |

- **Read left to right, as a person reads:** a statement uses its words, and the
  next starts after it. Read at every position, "es un animal so el gato" also
  gave animal —so→ gato.
- **A bare object** ("es dura") is what follows the relation, unless that word
  opens something new — a marker stands after it ("vive **so el** perro").
- **An article** — a marker with a partner in the other key (el/la, un/una) — is
  neither the subject nor the relation of a statement. A marker without a
  partner may relate: "es" stands before gendered words ("es bueno / es buena")
  and is still the relation of "Dios es amor".
- **A thing** (a word the text puts mostly after a marker) is never a relation:
  reading from "porque", "porque Dios es amor" became porque —Dios→ es and ate
  the real statement.
- Said once is an anecdote: walking uses links said twice.

## Thinking is walking

- **What it is:** from a word, follow the links of the question taught ("es" and
  its silhouette — imitation, P34) as far as they go; each node once.
- **What it inherits:** everything said of it and of what it reaches.
- **A family:** what two words both reach (P46, now by association).

## In the little world

Nothing says that a dog lives. Walking perro → animal, it inherits **vive** and
**come**; rex → perro → animal, two steps up, rex lives too. The table reaches
**madera** and inherits **dura**, and never reaches animal, never lives.

## On the New Testament

39,697 links read. What it walks to:

> **Dios** es: grande, bueno, amor, fiel, veraz, justo, cerca …
> Dios **de** Abraham, **de** Jacob, **de** Israel

> **Cristo** —es→ Dios, so Cristo is "de Abraham, de Jacob, de Israel"
> **(because it is Dios)**

No sentence says that of Cristo: it was reached by walking. That is the
associative inheritance the author asked for, on scripture.

The noise, plainly: "Dios es **que**", "Dios es **.**", "**los**" are read as
objects; through them the walk escapes into glue, and the family of Dios and
Cristo is inflated by it. Punctuation also stands as a relation (", Jesucristo"
— an apposition, sometimes real, often not). Señor, hombre and Ley reach nothing
by "es": the NT rarely says what they are.

## What had to be fixed in P44 for this to work

The map found nothing on the NT at first: **almost every word was a marker**
("Dios", "es", "porque", even "amor"). P44 asked which side a word points to when
it stands before a twin, never how often it does. Now:

- a marker stands before twins **more often than twins stand anywhere** (D50) —
  cut instead at the tall side of the share, "el" and "de" fell out, since few
  nouns have a twin;
- markers are found **twice**: some twins are markers themselves (una/uno,
  esta/esto), and what stands before a marker is not a marker — "es una" had made
  "es" one.

On the NT: Dios, porque, Jesús, amor, y are no markers; el, la, un, una, de, su
are; "es" is one too, honestly — it stands before gendered adjectives.

## P48 — The melody of the path (2026-10-12)

> Associations are diverse, and that is where it understands meaning — the
> difference between "Dios es grande" and "Dios es amor". If instead of storing
> every construction we store the melody of the path in the AMMC, we can
> generate meanings of meanings, combined meanings, more complex ones — and from
> there create senses, tools, things never thought of, falling like dominoes.
> — the author

`rz_assoc_tunes`, `rz_assoc_dominoes`; `razar dominoes [path] [relation]`.

- **Meaning is how far a word lets you walk.** "grande" is a leaf; "amor" is a
  place with its own paths ("el amor es paciente").
- **A tune** is the shape of a two-step walk, a —r1→ m —r2→ c, written (r1, r2).
  It is **heard** to lead to r3 when the text also says the shortcut a —r3→ c
  straight out. Heard twice, it is learned — a tool for thinking.
- **Dominoes.** A learned tune writes what nobody wrote: every path of its shape
  without a shortcut gets one, marked with its round. The new links make new
  paths; the next round falls from them, until nothing new falls. Fallen links
  are walked like said ones.

### In the little world

The text says rex is a dog, dogs are animals, animals are beings — and also "rex
es un animal" and "el perro es un ser": the tune **es · es → es** is heard.
Nothing says tom is an animal, nor that tom is a being:

- round 1: **tom es animal** falls;
- round 2: **tom es ser** falls — only because tom became an animal in round 1.

Meanings of meanings, and what the text said is never rewritten as fallen.

### On the New Testament and on Quiroga: no tune to learn yet

Learning on the first half of the NT, judged on the second:

- every relation let in, 2,962 tunes are heard twice — all of them glue
  ("Jerusalén · Los → los", ", · yo → ,"). 50,000 meanings fall; the second half
  says 160 of them against 108 for blind ends (1.5×), and what it confirms is
  glue: "de que los", "de los sumos";
- the tunes of the question taught ("es" and its silhouette): **none** is heard
  twice. Quiroga hears no tune at all.

To learn *es · es → es* a book has to say all three — X is Y, Y is Z, and X is Z
— and say them more than once. A narrative almost never does. The mechanism falls
like dominoes where a tune is heard; scripture and a novel do not teach the tune.

### Fixed in P44 on the way

- **"Both" only if seen before both sides**, twice each. The three-way split
  makes three groups even where there is no "both"; with few markers "la" landed
  in the middle and the keys broke (el paired with una).
- **An article is told by what follows it**: a marker of one side whose next
  word is mostly a thing. Told by having a partner in the other key, "una" —
  left without one — was read as a relation: "then una perra" became
  then —una→ perra, and the glue tunes came from there.

- **D110** — the glue tunes: relations that are punctuation or capitalised
  articles ("Los", "Y") should not be relations; an article at a sentence start
  is not yet told.
- **D108** — senses from the map: a node that many things reach ("animal",
  "vive") is a deep property; whether it should become a sense (P30).
