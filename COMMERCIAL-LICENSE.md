## Do you need a commercial license?

**You do NOT need one if:**

- You study the code, run it, modify it for yourself.
- You use it in academic research and publish what you do.
- You use it in a project of your own that is also AGPL.
- You teach with it.

**You DO need one if:**

- You want to put this code — or parts of it — into a **closed product** that
  you distribute without publishing your source.
- You want to **serve it over a network** (SaaS, API, an internal service
  others can reach) without publishing the complete source of your service.
  *This is the AGPL's own clause: section 13 requires handing the source even
  to someone who only interacts over the network.* An organism is exactly the
  kind of thing somebody serves rather than ships, so this case is the usual
  one here, not the exception.
- Your organisation has a policy against strong copyleft.
- You need warranties, support or indemnity that the AGPL explicitly denies.

Rule of thumb: **if your lawyer gets nervous about the AGPL, you need this
license.**

## What it grants

A commercial license replaces the AGPL's obligations with negotiated terms.
Typically:

| Point | AGPL-3.0 | Commercial |
|---|---|---|
| Publish your derived code | required | **no** |
| Hand source to network users (§13) | required | **no** |
| Use in a proprietary product | not allowed | **yes** |
| Price | free | by agreement |
| Support and warranty | none | by agreement |

The shape of the price is agreed case by case: one-off, annual subscription,
per installation, or a royalty on the revenue of the product that embeds it.

## What NO license here covers

This has to be said plainly, because it goes to the honesty of this
laboratory:

- **Ideas are not licensed.** How the organism works — that a switch of
  negation can be found by what it does rather than by its name, that a denial
  of what is mostly said raises a question, that senses can be grown instead of
  declared — is description, and it is free the minute it is published. Nobody
  needs permission to use it, and we could not grant it if they did.
- What IS licensed is the **code** (AGPL or commercial) and the **content** —
  the design notes and the autopsies — under
  [CC BY 4.0](LICENSE-CONTENT.txt).
- This license **sells no results and no conclusions.** It sells software and
  the right to integrate it without opening yours.
- It grants nothing over `feed/`. That material is not ours to license.

## What is here worth licensing

Each piece has its design note in `docs/design/`, with what it is and what it
measured.

- **The organism itself** — C11, no dependency beyond libm, ~9,000 lines with
  887 checks. It eats raw bytes, composes the pieces that recur, forgets
  without deleting, recalls, predicts before it reads, and grows its own
  senses.
- **Lexicon switches** — finding the word that negates by five measurable
  structural properties, against the text itself, naming nothing. See
  `docs/design/neurons.md`.
- **Closed doors and their questions** — holding what was said alongside the
  same place said denied, and asking why *this* time it went the other way.
  The rule is the majority of two counts and nothing else.
- **Conceptual memory** — nearest-concept recall over a perception vector, so
  that what changes how it sees changes what it decides.
- **Senses that grow** — organs that appear, are pruned by disuse and poor
  performance, and are never declared in advance.
- **Dreams, interest and the inner voice** — how it chooses what to work on
  while nothing is being asked of it.
